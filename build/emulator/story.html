The harbour

The boats came in before the storm. Every rope was checked twice, and the lamps along the quay were lit early.

Children ran between the nets while the old pilot counted hulls against the grey water.

The lighthouse

At the end of the breakwater the keeper climbed the stairs. The lens turned, slow and patient, and the beam went out across the bay.

Far off, a single sail turned toward the light.

The morning

By dawn the wind had dropped. The harbour was quiet, and the gulls were back on the posts.

The return

In the afternoon the small boat was pulled up the slipway. Its crew walked slowly along the quay, stopping at every door to thank someone.

The pilot took them to the inn, where the fire was still burning from the night, and ordered soup for everyone at the long table.

The evening

When the lamps were lit again the harbour looked the same as it had the day before, but everyone who walked past the boathouse looked in.

The keeper came down from the lighthouse for the first time in a week and sat by the window, listening to the stories of the storm.

The next day

The sail was mended on the quay in the sun. Children held the corners while the old sailmaker pushed the needle through the heavy cloth.

By evening the boat was in the water again, and the pilot added a line to his book about the weather, the wind and the light.
